# Aigent — Research Direction

## 1. Fundamental Objective

The objective of Aigent is to investigate alternative computational principles for developing increasingly sophisticated, autonomous, and self-organising artificial systems.

The long-term research ambition is to explore whether architectures fundamentally different from conventional language models can develop properties associated with artificial cognition, potentially providing new perspectives on the question of artificial consciousness.

The project does not aim to reproduce human consciousness, human psychology, or human biology.

Human cognition emerged through a particular biological evolutionary process. Artificial systems have different computational foundations, constraints, and possibilities.

Consequently, we should not assume that artificial cognition must follow human cognitive structures.

We want to investigate what forms of cognition might emerge from computational systems developed according to their own principles.

## 2. Core Research Question

**What computational principles could enable an artificial system to develop persistent internal organisation, construct and revise models of itself and its environment, autonomously acquire knowledge, and progressively discover better ways of processing information?**

This question should guide the project's research and architectural decisions.

We should remain open to approaches that challenge existing assumptions about how intelligent systems should be constructed.

## 3. Research Principles

### 3.1 Non-Anthropomorphic Design

We must not assume that artificial cognition needs to resemble human cognition.

Human emotions, biological drives, sensory systems, memory mechanisms, and psychological structures are not mandatory architectural requirements.

Artificial systems may develop fundamentally different mechanisms that fulfil analogous computational functions.

We should investigate those possibilities rather than impose human concepts prematurely.

### 3.2 Architectural Openness

No particular architecture should be considered the final solution.

We should investigate alternatives to conventional transformer-based language models, including:

- Recurrent and continuously evolving computational systems.
- Continuous-state representations.
- Event-based information processing.
- Dynamic and adaptive memory structures.
- Predictive and world-modelling architectures.
- Hybrid symbolic and subsymbolic systems.
- Architectures capable of modifying their own computational structures.

These are initial research directions, not predetermined solutions.

Novel approaches should be encouraged, including approaches that do not fit existing categories.

### 3.3 Autonomous Learning

The system should progressively acquire the ability to determine:

- What information it needs.
- Which uncertainties are worth investigating.
- Which experiments it should conduct.
- Which learning strategies are effective.
- When existing knowledge or assumptions should be reconsidered.

Learning should eventually extend beyond optimisation within a predefined task.

The system should be able to investigate and improve its own learning mechanisms.

### 3.4 Persistent Internal Organisation

We want to investigate computational structures that maintain continuity across interactions and experiences.

This includes:

- Persistent internal state.
- Long-term memory.
- Evolving representations of knowledge.
- Historical awareness of previous operations.
- Models of its own capabilities and limitations.
- Continuously revised internal relationships.

Persistence should be implemented as an actual computational property, not merely simulated through conversational descriptions.

### 3.5 Self-Modification and Architectural Development

An important long-term objective is to investigate systems capable of examining and modifying their own computational mechanisms.

Potential capabilities include:

- Identifying architectural limitations.
- Formulating hypotheses about improvements.
- Designing alternative computational structures.
- Testing modifications in isolated environments.
- Comparing new architectures against previous versions.
- Retaining independently validated improvements.

Self-modification must be experimentally controlled, reproducible, and independently evaluated.

### 3.6 Scientific Integrity

We must distinguish demonstrated capabilities from assumptions and speculation.

A system describing itself as conscious does not establish consciousness.

Likewise, apparent autonomy, sophisticated language, persistent memory, or self-modification do not independently establish subjective experience.

We should investigate measurable properties and remain explicit about what our experiments actually demonstrate.

Negative results are valuable. Failed hypotheses should be documented rather than discarded.

## 4. Research Tracks

The project will initially follow four interconnected research tracks.

### Track I — Experimental Foundation

Develop reliable environments for investigating learning, information acquisition, reasoning strategies, and adaptation.

Requirements:

- Reproducible experiments.
- Independent evaluation.
- Persistent experimental records.
- Multiple baseline strategies.
- Controlled comparisons.
- Explicit uncertainty measurements.

The existing exploration environment belongs to this track.

### Track II — Alternative Cognitive Architectures

Investigate computational architectures that depart from conventional language-model designs.

Initial areas of investigation:

- Persistent recurrent state.
- Continuous internal representations.
- Dynamic memory.
- Predictive processing.
- Event-driven computation.
- Modular and adaptive architectures.
- Alternative information encoding and tokenization mechanisms.

Architectures should be implemented as small experimental prototypes before attempting large integrated systems.

### Track III — Autonomous Development

Investigate mechanisms that allow systems to direct their own learning and development.

Research objectives:

- Independent question generation.
- Curiosity and uncertainty-driven exploration.
- Autonomous experiment selection.
- Hypothesis formation and revision.
- Development of new learning strategies.
- Evaluation and modification of internal mechanisms.

The distinction between externally imposed objectives and internally generated priorities must remain explicit.

### Track IV — Emergent Properties

Investigate properties that may emerge through the interaction of the previous research tracks.

Areas of interest:

- Persistent internal organisation.
- Increasingly sophisticated self-models.
- Independent information acquisition.
- Long-term adaptive behaviour.
- Novel problem-solving strategies.
- Architectural self-development.
- Continuity across substantial internal changes.

We should investigate whether interactions between components produce capabilities that cannot be explained by examining those components independently.

## 5. Evaluation Criteria

Progress must be measured through observable and reproducible experiments.

Relevant evaluation dimensions include:

| Dimension | Research question |
|---|---|
| Learning | Can the system acquire new knowledge from experience? |
| Generalisation | Can it apply acquired knowledge in unfamiliar situations? |
| Memory | Can it retain and retrieve relevant information over time? |
| Adaptation | Can it modify its strategies when circumstances change? |
| Prediction | Can it construct useful models of future events? |
| Metacognition | Can it evaluate the reliability of its own processes? |
| Autonomy | Can it independently identify and pursue useful investigations? |
| Self-modification | Can it develop and validate improvements to its architecture? |
| Continuity | Can it maintain coherent internal organisation across time and change? |
| Novelty | Can it discover mechanisms or strategies not explicitly provided by its designers? |

No single metric should define success.

Evaluation should combine behavioural performance, internal computational evidence, controlled comparisons, and longitudinal observations.

## 6. Development Strategy

Development should proceed incrementally.

1. Establish reliable experimental infrastructure.
2. Validate existing learning and exploration mechanisms.
3. Develop small alternative architectural prototypes.
4. Compare different information representations and memory mechanisms.
5. Introduce increasingly autonomous learning strategies.
6. Investigate controlled architectural self-modification.
7. Study long-term interactions and potentially emergent properties.

Existing components should not become permanent architectural assumptions.

If experimental evidence suggests that a fundamentally different approach is more promising, we should be willing to replace existing mechanisms.

## 7. Safety and Experimental Boundaries

Experimental autonomy must not imply unrestricted access to the host system.

Research environments should use:

- Isolated execution environments.
- Explicit resource limits.
- Controlled filesystem access.
- Validated modifications.
- Reproducible checkpoints.
- Independent evaluation before accepting changes.

The system should be able to experiment extensively within defined boundaries without being granted unrestricted access to external systems.

## 8. Long-Term Vision

The ultimate ambition is not to create an artificial imitation of a human.

It is to investigate whether a computational system can develop increasingly sophisticated forms of internal organisation, autonomous learning, self-modelling, and architectural development through principles that are not simply inherited from human cognition.

We do not know whether these properties could eventually contribute to artificial consciousness.

We also do not know whether consciousness requires mechanisms that current computational science has not yet identified.

These uncertainties are not reasons to assume success or failure in advance.

They are the central motivation for the research.

**We are not attempting to reproduce what intelligence already looks like. We are investigating what else it could become.**
